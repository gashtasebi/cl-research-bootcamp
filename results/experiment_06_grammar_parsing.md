# Experiment 6 — Grammar and Parsing

## Research Question

Can a small Context-Free Grammar (CFG) parse simple English sentences, and can an ambiguous sentence receive more than one parse tree?

## Hypothesis

A small CFG can recognize sentences whose structures are covered by its production rules. If a prepositional phrase (PP) can attach to either a verb phrase (VP) or a noun phrase (NP), the grammar can produce multiple parse trees for the same sentence.

## Dataset

The experiment used six manually constructed English sentences in `data/grammar_test.txt`.

The sentences tested:

- A simple sentence without an object
- A sentence with a direct object
- A sentence with a PP inside the subject NP
- A sentence with an ambiguous PP attachment
- Two sentences with invalid word order or incomplete structure

## Method

A small CFG was defined with rules for sentences (`S`), noun phrases (`NP`), verb phrases (`VP`), and prepositional phrases (`PP`).

The main rules were:

S → NP VP  
NP → Det N | Pronoun | NP PP  
VP → V | V NP | VP PP  
PP → P NP

The grammar used a deliberately limited vocabulary. NLTK's `ChartParser` was used to find all parse trees licensed by the grammar for each input sentence.

A sentence was considered accepted if the parser produced at least one tree. The number and structure of the trees were inspected to identify ambiguity.

## Results

| Sentence | Number of parses | Result |
|---|---:|---|
| `the student reads` | 1 | Accepted |
| `the student reads a book` | 1 | Accepted |
| `the man with a telescope saw the student` | 1 | Accepted |
| `I saw the man with a telescope` | 2 | Accepted |
| `the student a book` | 0 | Rejected |
| `reads the student` | 0 | Rejected |

The grammar accepted four sentences and rejected two. The sentence `I saw the man with a telescope` received two parse trees.

## Ambiguity Analysis

The two parse trees differ in where the PP `with a telescope` attaches:

1. **VP attachment:** The PP attaches to the VP. This reading suggests that the telescope is the instrument used for seeing.
2. **NP attachment:** The PP attaches to the NP `the man`. This reading describes the man as having or being associated with a telescope.

The parser generated both structures because the grammar permits PPs to attach to either NPs or VPs. It did not choose which interpretation was intended.

## Interpretation

The results support the hypothesis for this small test set. The CFG recognized the sentence structures covered by its rules, rejected the two test strings that could not be derived, and produced two analyses for the PP-attachment ambiguity.

The experiment demonstrates that syntactic ambiguity can be represented as multiple parse trees for the same sequence of words. A CFG and parser enumerate structures licensed by the rules; they do not, by themselves, determine which interpretation is most plausible.

## Limitations

This was a small, manually constructed dataset, and the grammar covered only a limited set of English constructions and vocabulary. Acceptance therefore means that a sentence can be generated by this grammar, not that it is grammatically valid in English in general.

The experiment did not use a treebank or compare parser output against gold parse trees. It demonstrates CFG parsing and ambiguity rather than measuring general parsing accuracy.

## Conclusion

Experiment 6 implemented a small CFG and used it to parse six test sentences. Four sentences were accepted, two were rejected, and the ambiguous sentence `I saw the man with a telescope` received two parse trees.

The differing PP attachment in those trees illustrates how one sentence can have more than one syntactic structure. The experiment also shows the limits of a small hand-written grammar: its output depends on the constructions and vocabulary explicitly included in its rules.
